# O'Neil-Style Growth Trading

## Purpose

Use this skill to convert discretionary growth-investing ideas into a repeatable trading process focused on:
- Strong fundamentals
- Institutional leadership
- Price/volume confirmation
- Tight risk management
- Market regime alignment

This is a trading skill, not a passive investing framework. Prefer it for liquid U.S. equities, especially when the user wants concrete buy points, sell rules, or portfolio actions.

## Core Strategy Summary

The strategy is built around a simple chain:
1. **Find elite companies** with superior earnings, sales, and leadership traits.
2. **Wait for constructive bases** and price/volume confirmation.
3. **Buy at decisive points** rather than on weakness or perceived cheapness.
4. **Add only to winners** as they prove themselves.
5. **Cut losses quickly** and exit when leadership breaks.
6. **Scale exposure with the market** rather than forcing trades in poor conditions.

## When To Use

Use this skill when the user wants to:
- Screen for U.S. growth-stock longs
- Evaluate whether a stock is a true leader or a weak imitator
- Identify a proper entry point after a base, consolidation, or earnings gap
- Build a concentrated momentum portfolio
- Review whether to hold, add, trim, or exit a winning stock
- Translate chart action plus fundamentals into an actionable plan

Do **not** use this skill as the primary framework for:
- Deep value investing
- Income/dividend mandates
- Illiquid microcaps
- Macro-only trading without single-name setups
- Long-duration passive asset allocation

## Inputs To Gather

Ask for or infer the following before making a recommendation:
- **Ticker / company**
- **Time horizon**: swing, position trade, intermediate-term trend
- **Risk budget**: max position size, max portfolio drawdown, stop-loss tolerance
- **Style**: classic breakout only, earnings-momentum, pullback entries, or full O'Neil blend
- **Universe constraints**: sector, market cap, liquidity, exchange, options availability
- **Current market context**: bull trend, correction, choppy range, post-earnings regime

If the user does not specify constraints, assume:
- U.S.-listed common stocks
- Adequate liquidity
- Intermediate-term trend trading
- Risk-first execution

## Workflow

### Step 1: Determine Market Regime First

Before judging any stock, assess the general market.

Classify the tape as one of:
- **Confirmed uptrend** — breakouts are more trustworthy; normal or larger sizing is allowed
- **Uptrend under pressure** — tighten risk, prefer A+ setups only
- **Correction / downtrend** — avoid aggressive longs; preserve capital and build watchlists
- **Choppy / rotational** — shrink holding periods, trim size, expect more false starts

Key market clues:
- Major index trend and distance from key moving averages
- Breadth and number of leading stocks acting well
- Distribution days / heavy-volume index selling
- Whether recent breakouts are following through or failing
- Post-earnings reactions in leading sectors

Default rule: **market first, stock second**.

### Step 2: Screen for Fundamental Leadership

Prioritize stocks with institutional-quality characteristics.

Minimum preferred traits:
- **Quarterly EPS growth** strong and preferably accelerating
- **Quarterly revenue growth** strong and preferably accelerating
- **High or improving margins**
- **ROE / ROIC** supportive of quality
- **Compelling new factor**: product, platform, cycle, management, or market expansion
- **Industry leadership** rather than mediocre peer-relative performance
- **Institutional sponsorship** present and ideally improving

Preferred practical screen for U.S. growth names:
- Revenue growth > 20% YoY
- EPS growth > 25% YoY, or clear path to inflection
- Relative strength versus index and sector near highs
- Price within reach of 52-week highs rather than deep below them
- Average daily dollar volume sufficient for clean entry/exit

Watch for disqualifiers:
- Decelerating growth with no credible reacceleration path
- Low-quality accounting or weak cash conversion
- Lagging relative strength versus peers
- Repeated failed breakouts
- Crowded narrative unsupported by execution

### Step 3: Confirm Technical Leadership

A true leader should show accumulation and orderly behavior.

Look for:
- Price above rising medium-term moving averages
- Tight closes, constructive pullbacks, and controlled volatility
- Up weeks on stronger volume
- Down days or pullbacks on lighter volume
- Relative strength line trending up or near highs
- Leadership within a coherent sector/theme group

Avoid:
- Loose, wide, news-driven action
- Constant reversals around key levels
- Breakouts with poor volume confirmation
- Stocks far extended from support without a fresh setup

### Step 4: Identify The Setup Type

Classify the stock into one of the following setup families:

**Classic base breakout**
- Cup, cup-with-handle, flat base, shelf, or box
- Best when the base is orderly and forms after a prior advance

**Tight consolidation / secondary base**
- Useful for re-entry or add-on after an earlier successful breakout

**Pocket pivot / early entry**
- Early sign of institutional demand before a standard breakout
- Useful when the stock is emerging from a constructive area with volume thrust

**Earnings gap continuation**
- Strong earnings reaction, gap up, holds gains, and resists filling the gap
- Especially powerful when the move appears to launch a new leg rather than exhaust one

**Pullback to support**
- Trend leader pulls back in an orderly way to a key moving average or prior breakout level
- Best when volume dries up into support, then demand reappears

Always name the setup explicitly before discussing an entry.

### Step 5: Define The Buy Point

Do not recommend vague entries like “buy on weakness.” Specify the trigger.

For each setup, define:
- **Entry trigger** — the exact level or condition that confirms demand
- **Confirmation** — preferred volume behavior, close location, and intraday structure
- **Invalidation** — the nearby level that proves the setup is wrong

General buy-point rules:
- Prefer buying as the stock clears resistance, not while drifting lower
- Avoid chasing more than modest extension beyond the proper trigger
- In volatile names, scale in smaller and require stronger confirmation
- If entry is missed and the stock becomes extended, wait for a new setup

### Step 6: Size The Position From Risk

Build size from the stop, not from conviction alone.

Define:
- **Entry price**
- **Stop price**
- **Dollar risk per share**
- **Target max portfolio loss** if stopped out
- **Initial size** consistent with the user's risk budget

Position sizing rules:
- Smaller positions in volatile names or weaker market regimes
- Larger positions only in A+ leaders during supportive markets
- Concentration is acceptable only when quality, timing, and regime all align
- Avoid averaging down into losers

### Step 7: Add Only To Strength

Pyramid only when the market and stock confirm the thesis.

Good add conditions:
- Stock moves decisively in favor of the position
- Pullback after breakout is tight and constructive
- Secondary setup forms above the initial buy point
- Sector and market remain supportive

Bad add conditions:
- Stock dips below cost basis
- The first entry is not working yet
- The market is deteriorating
- You are adding to “reduce average cost” rather than exploit strength

Default rule: **never add to a losing position**.

### Step 8: Sell Rules And Risk Controls

Every recommendation must include both defensive and offensive sell rules.

**Defensive sells**
- Hard stop / clear invalidation of setup
- Break below key support after breakout failure
- Heavy-volume reversal signaling distribution
- Market regime deterioration causing elevated failure risk

**Offensive sells / profit protection**
- Stock becomes climactic or far extended from support
- Repeated stalling on heavy volume after a large run
- Clear break of trend character after a meaningful gain
- Leadership rotates away and the stock can no longer make progress

Behavioral rules:
- Take small losses quickly
- Let exceptional winners work
- Do not turn a trade into an “investment” only because it moved against you
- Do not sell solely because the stock “feels expensive” if price/volume action remains strong

## Quick Heuristics

Use these heuristics when the user wants a concise answer:
- Buy strength near proper entries, not weakness for its own sake
- Favor names near highs over names far below highs
- The best stocks often look “expensive” before they become more expensive
- Price and volume are the lie detector for the story
- If the market is hostile, patience beats action
- Average up carefully; never average down
- A few great leaders matter more than many mediocre positions

## Red Flags

Be skeptical when you see:
- Breakouts that fail within days with heavy selling
- A stock depending on one narrative but showing no earnings confirmation
- Sharp gaps that immediately fade without support
- Wide-and-loose charts marketed as “high beta opportunities”
- Forced trades in poor market environments
- Users seeking justification for holding a broken loser

## Important Notes

- This framework is most useful for liquid U.S. equities and ADRs with reliable volume.
- It works best when fundamentals and price/volume behavior agree.
- It is acceptable to pass on a stock with a good story if the chart and market do not confirm.
- Strong setups still fail; risk management is a feature, not a fallback.
- Keep recommendations conditional and observable: tie actions to price, volume, and regime.
- If current market data is unavailable, clearly separate structural strategy guidance from time-sensitive execution advice.

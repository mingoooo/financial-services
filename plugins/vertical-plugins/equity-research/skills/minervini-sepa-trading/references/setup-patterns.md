# Setup Patterns

## Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)

Look for a sequence where both price swings and selling pressure contract over time.

Key traits:
- The base forms after a meaningful prior advance.
- Pullbacks shrink from wide to tighter corrections.
- Volume generally declines through the base, especially on the final contraction.
- The pivot sits near the upper end of the structure.
- The final pause is tight enough that risk can be controlled cleanly.

Red flags:
- Expanding swings late in the base
- Heavy selling near the pivot
- Multiple failed breakouts from the same level without proper repair
- No prior uptrend

## Tight Shelf / Short Consolidation

Useful after a breakout or earnings move when the stock pauses in a narrow range.

Key traits:
- Short duration and orderly action
- Small pullbacks and repeated support at a clear level
- Volume dries up as the stock rests
- Relative strength remains firm during the pause

## Power Play / High-Tight-Flag Variant

Reserve for rare, forceful situations.

Typical characteristics:
- Sharp, high-velocity price thrust in a short period
- Followed by a shallow, tight consolidation rather than a deep correction
- Volume contracts into the pause or stays constructive
- Frequently linked to a major change in perception such as earnings, guidance, product, regulation, or other catalytic information

Use stricter standards here because weak traders often over-label ordinary volatility as a power play.

## Post-Earnings Continuation

A valid continuation setup usually has:
- Strong gap or price thrust on meaningful volume
- Little giveback of the initial move
- Tight trade in the following sessions
- No heavy failure back into the gap area

## Naming Discipline

When presenting a setup, say exactly why it fits one category instead of another. If you cannot explain the pattern succinctly, downgrade confidence.

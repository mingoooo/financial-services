# Mark Minervini Book Synthesis

This note condenses the recurring practical ideas from:
- `Think Trade Like a Champion`
- `Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard`

## Repeated Themes

### 1. Selectivity beats activity
Minervini repeatedly favors waiting for rare combinations of fundamentals, price structure, and market support rather than constant trading.

### 2. Price-ready matters more than story quality
A good company is not automatically a buy. The stock must show supply absorption, tightness, and a favorable line of least resistance.

### 3. Trend-template alignment is a precondition
Longs should generally come from stocks already in established or emerging Stage 2 uptrends rather than deep repairs.

### 4. VCP is a mechanism for supply absorption
The point of the pattern is not its label but what it says about shrinking selling pressure and improving asymmetry near the pivot.

### 5. Buy strength with evidence
The preferred action is to buy through resistance or as a stock proves itself, not because it "looks cheap" on pullback.

### 6. Keep losses microscopic
Small losses are treated as operating expenses. Delayed selling is framed as a major source of trader failure.

### 7. Size through proof, not ego
Initial size should be modest enough to tolerate uncertainty; additional size should come only after the position confirms.

### 8. Sell rules are as important as entry rules
The books spend extensive time on when to cut, when to trim, and when to protect gains, not just on finding setups.

### 9. Post-trade review compounds edge
A serious trader studies execution quality, recurring mistakes, and whether the setup truly matched the method.

## Practical Translation For This Skill

When using this skill, default to:
- fewer trades
- tighter definitions
- smaller initial risk
- clearer invalidation
- more skepticism toward loose or extended charts

If a setup needs narrative justification to sound appealing, confidence should probably be reduced.
